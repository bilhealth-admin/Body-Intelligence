# Scientific and health-safety rules

BIL is a tracking and decision-support product, not a medical device. It does
not diagnose, treat, prescribe, or infer body composition from scale weight.

- Deterministic engines remain authoritative for BMR, TDEE, nutrition targets,
  hydration targets, trends, scores, and scenarios.
- Formula assumptions and evidence are shown with outputs.
- Missing observations lower confidence and are named explicitly.
- Plateau and water-retention messages are cautious hypotheses and never claim
  causation from correlation.
- A single large scale change is not labeled fat or muscle change.
- Goal-date estimates require sufficient history and remain ranges, not
  promises.
- Recommendations do not reward starvation, extreme deficits, dehydration, or
  fastest weight loss.
- Experiments are personal observations with adherence, limitations, and at
  most moderate confidence; they are not medical proof.
- AI, when a verified backend is later configured, may explain deterministic
  results but cannot silently log data or change targets. Every write requires
  explicit confirmation.
- Pregnancy, eating-disorder history, clinician-directed diets, symptoms, and
  medical emergencies require qualified professional care rather than app
  inference.

Health-related copy must remain supportive, specific about evidence, and free
of manipulative certainty or shame.
